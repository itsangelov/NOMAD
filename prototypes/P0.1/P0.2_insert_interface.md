## 2. Insert geometry requirements

**NOMAD / P0.2 interface requirements** (these are our requirements, not manufacturer data):
- M3 × 0.5 internal thread, brass, knurled, heat-set type for thermoplastics, installable with a soldering iron.
- Standard catalogue part from more than one maker.
- Length ≤ 6.0 mm, so it fits the coupons without changing their geometry.
- Pull-out need at the worst P0.2 joint (the rail ear's far screw):
  - ~56 N at the R2 0.9 kg load (D.1 pad model);
  - ~120 N if the optional 2 kg R2 extension is run.
  - The B frame screws carry only a few N.

**Manufacturer-specific dimensions** (must come from the datasheet of the insert actually bought; not invented here):

| Quantity | What public sources show (as quoted) | Status for P0.2 |
|---|---|---|
| Body length | The widely sold "standard" M3 inserts for 3D printing are **M3 × 5.7** (Prusa "M3 standard", CNC Kitchen "M3 × 5.7") | **datasheet** |
| Outside diameter | General guidance **4.0–5.0 mm**; one reference lists 4.6 mm | **datasheet** |
| Recommended hole | Stated values differ: several sources give **4.0 mm** for M3 in PLA/PETG/ABS; a general guide gives **4.2–4.6 mm**; Prusa says only "just large enough so the insert can be easily pushed in" and refers to a drawing | **datasheet** |
| Hole depth | Not given numerically by the sources checked | **derived below** |
| Boss OD | General guidance **8.0–9.0 mm** | guidance only |
| Installation temperature | Prusa: **printing temperature + 10 °C** | guidance only |

- **Conclusion:** a Ø4.0 pilot **cannot be assumed correct for every M3 insert**; published values span 4.0–4.6.
- A printed hole that is **too small is correctable** by drilling it up with a common hand drill bit. A hole that is too large is not.

**Derived coupon requirements** (*inferred / general practice*):

| Quantity | Requirement | How it is set |
|---|---|---|
| Hole diameter as modeled | the **smallest** plausible pilot (Ø4.0); final size = datasheet value, drilled after printing | FDM holes usually print slightly undersize (inferred), which also favours starting small |
| Hole depth | insert length + **~1 mm** relief for displaced melt and so the screw can't bottom | 5.7 + 1.3 = **7.0** |
| Wall around the insert | ≥ ~1.5–2 mm of printed material (≥ 4 perimeters at 0.4) | from the 8–9 mm boss guidance: (8 − 5)/2 = 1.5 |
| Edge distance (centre to free edge) | ≥ ~4 mm (≈ insert radius + wall) | derived |
| Screw engagement | ≥ ~1.5 × d = **4.5 mm** into the insert, and screw tip short of the hole bottom | general practice for full-thread strength (inferred) |

## 3. Practical insert styles considered

| | A. Straight knurled heat-set (the common "M3 × 5.7 standard" class, often with a tapered lead) | B. Flanged heat-set |
|---|---|---|
| Pull-out | Carried by the knurl in re-solidified plastic; depends on hole fit and print quality | Same knurl; the flange also stops pull-**through** toward the flange side |
| Rotation (torque-out) | Knurl; usually adequate for M3 at hand torque (inferred) | Same, plus flange friction |
| Installation | Simplest: press flush with the iron; a slight taper self-centres | The flange must seat flat; still simple |
| Printed boss | ~8–9 mm (guidance) | The same, **plus** a counterbore for the flange, or the flange sits proud |
| Thin walls | OK above ~1.5–2 mm of wall | The flange spreads load on thin skins |
| Availability | Very common, many makers | Common, fewer sizes |
| Repeated service | Good if not overheated; spinning is the typical failure | Similar; the flange resists being pushed in by the screw |

**C.** No third style has a strong mechanical case at P0.2 loads (≤ ~120 N against typical M3 insert pull-out in the hundreds of N, inferred), so it is not developed. A captive hex-nut pocket would avoid heat-setting, but it changes the geometry.

**For the P0.2 fixture specifically,** the evidence supports **A (straight, non-flanged)**:
- In both coupons the insert face **is** a test-critical seating plane:
  - the rail ear underside sets the 1.0 mm display gap on the 6.0 stub;
  - the B-frame shelf top sets the plate seat and the 2.0 / 1.0 mm clearance references.
- A flange would sit proud and shift those planes (typically by ~0.5–1 mm; the exact figure is datasheet-dependent). Avoiding that needs counterbores, which is a geometry change the loads don't justify.
- This is a fixture choice, not a NOMAD standard.

## 4. Recommended P0.2 interface

- **Diameter: keep Ø4.0 as modeled.** It is the lower bound of the published pilots, so it can only be too small, and too small is fixed by drilling up to the datasheet value after printing (§7). Moving to a larger nominal hole (e.g. 4.4) would suit some inserts and leave the 4.0-class inserts loose, with no fix.
- **Depth: change 6.0 → 7.0.** With the common 5.7 mm insert, a 6.0 blind hole leaves only 0.3 mm for displaced plastic. Melt then collects under or inside the insert, and the insert tends to seat high. A 7.0 hole gives ~1.3 mm of relief and keeps screw tips clear. There is ample material in both coupons, so neither is weakened (§5, §9).
- **Wall and edge distances:** unchanged; all exceed the requirement (§5).

## 7. Installation procedure

*No special equipment; a soldering iron with a conical, flat or insert tip.*

1. **Print the part** as oriented (100 % solid for the rails; frame per the P0.2 plan, with ≥ 4 perimeters around the insert holes).
2. **Inspect the hole.**
   - Clean strings from the bore.
   - Try the insert by hand: its lead or small end should enter about 0.5–1 mm and stop.
   - **If the datasheet pilot is larger than 4.0**, drill to that size by hand or at low speed (e.g. a 4.2 or 4.5 bit), to 7 mm depth. Tape on the bit makes a depth mark.
   - If the insert drops fully in by hand, the hole is too large (§8).
3. **Heat the insert.**
   - Set the iron to about the PETG printing temperature + 10 °C (Prusa's guidance). For typical PETG that is ~240–260 °C; start at the low end.
   - Place the insert on the hole, small end down, and put the iron tip into its thread bore.
4. **Seat it.**
   - Press **gently and straight down**; let the heat do the work. It should sink in ~5–10 s.
   - Stop when the top is ~0.2–0.3 mm **proud** of the surface.
5. **Flush and cool.**
   - Remove the iron. Immediately press the insert flush with a flat metal object (the side of a wrench or a steel ruler), which also squares it.
   - Hold ~10 s. Let it cool for ≥ 2 min before loading.
6. **Check alignment.** Thread a screw in by hand a few turns. It should stand perpendicular (check against a square or the caliper's beam), and the insert face should be flush (feel with a fingernail or a straightedge).
7. **Install the screw.** Snug, by hand with a normal driver, no power tools. Record what was used.
8. **Check engagement.** The screw should turn freely to full depth without hitting bottom (§6), and the insert must not turn or lift. Mark a witness line.

**Avoid:**
- **Overheating PETG:** don't linger, and don't heat above what's needed. Signs: bubbling, stringy ooze or a glossy crater around the hole.
- **Pushing too deep:** stop proud, then flush with a flat tool. Never push with the iron past flush.
- **Tilting:** keep the part flat on the table and the iron vertical. Heat-setting from the ear underside means the rail lies floor-up on the table.
- **Blocking the thread:** don't let molten plastic enter the thread from the top.

**Practice** on a spare hole or offcut first.

## 8. Failure modes

| Problem | Symptom | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Hole too small | Insert needs heavy force; a large melt ring; tends to tilt | Drill to the datasheet pilot before installing. Already installed: heat and pull it out with the iron + a screw, drill, reinstall. |
| Hole too large | Insert drops in; weak or no grip; spins | Add a drop of epoxy (becomes non-serviceable), use a larger insert, or reprint the part. Prevented by modeling the smallest pilot. |
| Insert too deep | Face below the seating surface; the screw clamps the part, not the insert, and loads pull it out | Reheat and pull up to flush with a screw threaded in, using pliers on the screw, not the insert. If it won't hold, reprint. |
| Insert tilted | Screw enters crooked; the seat rocks | While still warm, reheat and square it with a flat tool. Otherwise extract and reinstall. Drill out if the bore is damaged. |
| Boss cracks | A visible crack or whitening from the hole | Stop; the part is unusable for load data. Reprint with more perimeters or a hotter, slower insertion. |
| Excessive melting | Crater, ooze into the thread, a soft, distorted seat | Clear the thread with an M3 tap or screw once cold. If the seat is distorted (affects the 1.0 / 2.0 references), reprint. Lower the temperature next time. |
| Screw bottoms out | Screw stops before clamping; the insert lifts | Use the §6 lengths. Check nothing blocks the 7.0 hole bottom (melt); a shorter screw or a washer is the fix. |
| Insert spins on removal | Screw won't come out; the insert turns | Hold the insert (pliers on a flange isn't possible; press it down with a screwdriver on its rim while unscrewing) or reheat lightly with the screw in and pull it. Record as a service failure (P0.2 insert cycle test). |
