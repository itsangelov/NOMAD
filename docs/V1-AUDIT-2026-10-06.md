# NOMAD Virtual V1 — Pre-purchase Engineering Audit (2026-10-06)

**Scope:** an adversarial review of the Virtual V1 baseline (`docs/V1-VIRTUAL-INTEGRATION.md`, proposed D-011) before any money is spent. No architecture was changed, and B1 / D-010 is untouched.

**Changes made by this audit** (the minimum needed to stop real fragilities from hiding behind PASS results):
1. `CAD/tools/v1_integration.py`:
   - New WARN check: the display-hinge torque variant fails if the estimated lid / leaf masses are 25 % heavy.
   - New check: GPIO connector height on the HAT+. Vertical Dupont housings hit the deck, so the harness spec is now right-angle housings.
2. `prototypes/V1/v1_power.py`: prints the 6 h runtime margin in watts.

**Result:** 58 PASS / 8 WARN / 0 FAIL. The P0.1 and E-POWER regression outputs are unchanged.

## 3. Battery audit (Molicel M65A)

| Check | Finding |
|---|---|
| Data in the repo | Datasheet **v0.1, preliminary**: 23.0 Wh min, 6.4 Ah, 74.5 g, 25 mΩ DC, 26 A continuous, charge 0–60 °C. **Cycle life not stated.** The end-of-life factor 0.80 is borrowed from the 50E (≥ 80 % after 500 cycles) |
| 4S1P | Sound. Battery current 0.6 A NORMAL / 1.9 A WORST at cutoff: 14 × margin on the cell rating and 3 × on the charger's 6 A. Buck-boost charging is required for the P108 (hot Vmp ≈ 16.1 V < 16.8 V), and the BQ25798 provides it |
| Voltage | 16.8 / 14.4 / 12.8 (software cutoff) / 11.2 (BMS UV). The 12 V buck-boost covers the 12 V crossing. The 5 V buck has ample headroom |
| Energy | 92 Wh nominal; 82.8 usable (× 0.90 ASSUMED window); 66.2 at end of life (× 0.80 ASSUMED) |
| Thermal | I²R 0.06 W NORMAL / 0.41 W WORST: negligible. Charging at 3.2 A ≈ 0.5 C. Cells are in a separate bay, ≈ 39 °C EST while charging at 35 °C ambient |
| BMS | On-pack 4S protector (BQ7791501-class: OV 4.25, UV 2.8, OCD / OCC / SCD, NTC, 50 mA balancing) + 10 A MINI fuse (1 kA vs 119 A prospective). Adequate. A **custom pack PCB** is a second custom board; a reputable off-the-shelf 4S protection board with the same thresholds is a legitimate prototype simplification |
| Charger compatibility | 4S default on the BQ25798; JEITA from the pack NTC. Compatible (MFR) |
| Fit | Holder pack 91.6 × 80 × 28.9 in a 93.6 × 82 × 31 bay. The 50E (21.25 × 70.8) fits the same holder |
| Safety | Standard 4S Li-ion: spot-welded, never soldered; the fuse is replaceable; the pack is self-protected when removed. Safety is fine **if** a competent assembler builds the pack |
| **Real weakness** | The runtime margin, not the cell. The 6 h at end-of-life target survives only **1.04 W** more NORMAL load. Pi at 5.5 W (third-party video-playback figure) → 6.2 h; display at full brightness → 6.3 h. Either one fails the 6 h + 10 % reserve rule. A cell 8 % short on capacity still passes (6.8 h) |

**Verdict: YES, but only after:**
1. The NORMAL load is measured at the compute and display gates. If NORMAL at the battery is > 10 W, no 21700 cell in this bay meets 6 h at end of life; the target or the bay becomes the owner question, not the cell.
2. Five cells from a traceable distributor are capacity-tested on arrival.

The 50E remains the drop-in fallback, with the same holder. The preliminary datasheet is acceptable for a prototype. It is not acceptable for a frozen design.

## 5. Thermal audit

| Element | Basis | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Heat 6.8 / 15.6 W | CALC from the power model | Credible (all input becomes heat) |
| Fan flow 50 % of 5.5 CFM | ASSUMED; Sunon static pressure not read; `VENT_INTAKE_AREA` is **not used** by the model (a disconnected parameter) | The weakest input |
| Sensitivity | At 25 % of free air: NORMAL rise 10.2 K (HAT+ limit at 39.8 °C ambient); HIGH 15.1 K (34.9 °C) | NORMAL stays safe at 35 °C even with a poor fan; HIGH becomes marginal |
| Passive loss 0.3 W/K | EST | Second order |
| SoC 4.55 K/W | Inferred from one Raspberry Pi blog figure (64 °C, bench ambient ASSUMED 24 °C, wall power 8.8 W) | ±30 % plausible |
| Active Cooler under the HAT+ | Raspberry Pi's own supported stack (MFR) | Fine |
| M.2 HAT+ 50 °C ambient | MFR | It sits in the Active Cooler's exhaust, so local air is hotter than the bay average. **Measure** |
| Inlet / exhaust | Rear grille 120 × 13.5 + underside grille → under-keyboard → Pi bay → 30 mm exhaust in the right wall | Coherent single path; the battery bay is outside it |
| Battery isolation | Separate bay, bulkhead, own door | Good |

**Verdict:** the HIGH throttling above ≈ 31 °C is a **realistic limitation, not a V1 problem**.
- NOMAD's normal workload is far below sustained stress.
- Throttling is graceful.
- NORMAL operation keeps the HAT+ inside its limit to ≈ 40–43 °C ambient even if the fan is half as effective as assumed.

**No cooling redesign is justified. Simplest improvements, only if measured necessary:**
1. A printed baffle that makes the exhaust fan draw through the Active Cooler.
2. Fan at 100 % above 70 °C SoC.

Cheapest de-risking option (gate 0, free): check the EVM's size and mounting. If it fits the 90 × 45 × 16 power zone, or a relocated zone, **use the EVM as the V1 power board** and defer the custom PCB to V1.1.

## 10. Solar audit (B1 not reopened)

- **Electrically compatible:** P108 → BQ25798 buck-boost VOC-MPPT. Cable loss is negligible.
- **Daily figures are plausible as model outputs** (PVGIS, Sofia, 35° fixed, panel out all day, × 0.85 chain), but they are an **upper bound for real use**. The leaf is deployed only while stationary and aimed. At 30–60 % deployment, an annual mean of ≈ 10–20 Wh/day is a more honest planning number.
- **Solar is not critical to NOMAD's basic operation.** NOMAD runs entirely from the battery and recharges from USB-C. Solar is supplementary replenishment, and every requirement and runtime figure is met without it.

## 12. Mass / CG / stability audit

- **Total:** 3.03 kg, range 2.52–3.54 kg. Only 33 % is manufacturer-published.
- **Largest uncertainties:**
  - printed parts (1 233 g EST): lid core 411, base shell 355, panels 261, trays 141
  - display 170
  - trackpad 90
  - pack hardware 60
- **Tipping conclusions hold.** With the stay, a light base and heavy lid still give ≥ 11.8 m/s (worst: leaf upright). Without the stay, the worst rearward pose (φ 135, ψ 135) has **−3 mm margin**: it tips by gravity alone. The stay is already mandatory, so nothing changes.
- **The hinge choice does not survive the uncertainty** (W8).
- **Weigh first:**
  1. a printed lid core, or even a printed 100 × 100 sample of the lid skin, to calibrate the printed-mass model; the slicer gives the mass for free once the CAD exists
  2. the display (on arrival)
  3. the keyboard (MFR value: confirm)

### Answers

1. **Is V1 fundamentally sound?** Yes. Nothing found contradicts B1, the 4S1P power architecture, the placement or the envelope. The weaknesses are input data and thin margins, not architecture.
2. **Is anything a genuine blocker?** Not for the cheap gates. The display chain and the display hinges are blocked as **purchases** until gate 0 data and weighing exist.
3. **Is €1 405 justified, and can it be reduced?** As a parts list, it is roughly honest. Realistic total cash is ≈ €1 750 (≈ €1 900–2 100 with a print service). The only material reductions without compromise are EU sourcing (−€30–40) and using the EVM as the V1 power board (−€60 plus design time). The old €500–650 target was unrealistic for this specification.
4. **Is M65A acceptable?** Yes for the prototype, after a capacity test and the measured-load check. The cell is not the risk; the 1 W runtime margin is.
5. **Is the display sufficiently defined?** No. Not for CAD and not for purchase. It is a gate-0 data problem, not yet a reason to change displays.
6. **Is thermal acceptable?** Yes for the intended workload. NORMAL stays inside the HAT+ limit at 35 °C even with half the assumed fan flow. Sustained HIGH throttles gracefully above ≈ 31 °C. Measure before changing anything.
7. **Is 306 × 253 × 40 justified?** Yes. Width comes from the keyboard, depth from the trackpad + keyboard + rear strip, height from the battery and Pi stack. A thinner keyboard would not reduce the height.
8. **Highest-risk subsystem?** The custom power board: the charger, the PD controller and their Li-ion behaviour. The display is the highest *definition / sourcing* risk, but it is cheap to resolve.
9. **Cheapest test with the most information?** The SB-261 coupon plus a printed / weighed lid-skin sample (≈ €60–80). It tests B1's core mechanical risk, insert pull-out and printed-mass reality in one print. The next best is a USB-C power meter on the Pi (≈ €20 once the Pi is bought), which settles the runtime margin.
